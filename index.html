Research Projects

Cardiac Virtual Cell Modeling: Multimodal Data, Biophysical Mechanisms, and AI
PhD Research Project · Aug 2025 - Present
University of Macau

Integrating public single-cell/single-nucleus transcriptomics, fluorescence imaging, and multiscale biophysical models to develop a virtual cell framework combining mechanistic and AI/data-driven approaches for predicting cardiac cell states and dynamic responses.

  • Single-cell/single-nucleus data analysis: Reanalyzed public human atrial snRNA-seq data using Seurat normalization, Harmony batch correction, cell-subtype annotation, differential expression, and pseudobulk analysis to characterize cell states and disease-associated features.
  • Fluorescence imaging and wet-lab work: Conducting cell culture, staining, electrical stimulation, and functional assays, including Fluo-4 calcium fluorescence and Di-8-ANEPPS membrane-potential imaging; analyzing ROI signals and fluorescence time series to quantify cellular dynamics.
  • Multiscale mechanistic modeling: Coupled ODE systems for ion-channel electrophysiology, subcellular calcium regulation, and sarcomere contraction to model intracellular Ca²⁺ dynamics and active force generation.
  • Mechano-electric feedback and arrhythmia mechanisms: Incorporated stretch-activated currents, mechanical feedback, and spatially heterogeneous calcium buffering; simulated calcium waves and delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs) across pacing frequencies.
  • Parameter analysis and multimodal calibration: Performed Sobol global sensitivity analysis to quantify parameter effects on calcium transients and arrhythmia triggers; studying how transcriptomic features and imaging time series can constrain model parameters and support comparison with experimental observations.
  • AI-mechanistic integration: Investigating Neural ODEs, neural-network/ODE hybrid models, and multimodal data fusion for data-driven parameter estimation, cell-state and perturbation-response prediction, and iterative model refinement.
Results:
  • Presentation accepted at the 17th World Congress on Computational Mechanics & 10th European Congress on Computational Methods in Applied Sciences and Engineering (WCCM-ECCOMAS 2026), Munich, Germany, Jul 2026.
